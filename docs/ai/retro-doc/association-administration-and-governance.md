## What it does

This domain describes proposed capabilities for administering an association’s resources, partnerships, decisions, finances, documents, grants, data-protection duties, audit records, and legal obligations. Its specification sources range from inventory and sponsorship management (`specs/006`–`007`) through voting, executive oversight, and automation (`specs/008`–`011`), accounting (`specs/012`), document generation and grants (`specs/016`–`017`), GDPR and audit (`specs/019`–`020`), and the legal API (`specs/023-module-juridique/`).

These sources are feature specifications, not a verified map of shipped code. The only concrete application path they establish for this domain is the planned standalone `apps/api-juridique/` NestJS application; most other sources describe proposed components, collections, services, and API routes without confirming their implementation locations.

## Architecture as designed

- **Operational resources and partnerships:** Inventory covers equipment, reservations, loans, condition records, and maintenance, scoped by `authGroupId`. Partnership management relates partner records to contracts, commitments (counterparties), proof of delivery, visibility reports, and sponsor display settings. The specifications propose distinct services, collections, and API routes for these functions (`specs/006-gestion-stocks-inventaire/spec/spec.md`; `specs/007-partenariats-sponsors/spec/spec.md`).
- **Decisions and oversight:** Votes and polls are separate: official votes support eligibility, quorum, secret ballots, elections, and proxies; informal polls return immediate results without quorum or proxies. Vote outcomes and meeting information feed minutes. The executive dashboard is designed to aggregate membership, finance, events, and engagement KPIs, alerts, reports, trends, and role-oriented layouts (`specs/008-votes-sondages/spec/spec.md`; `specs/009-tableau-bord-executif/spec/spec.md`).
- **Automation:** A workflow definition stores its trigger and ordered actions separately from its execution history. Proposed triggers include events, schedules, conditions, and webhooks; actions include notifications, email, tasks, updates, delays, and conditions. Execution records retain trigger, state, steps, and context (`specs/011-workflows-automatises/spec/spec.md`).
- **Finance and funding:** Advanced accounting extends financial operations with configurable account mapping and exports, statement imports and suggested matching, invoice OCR with human review, and expense approval. Grant management follows funding opportunities into application drafting, review, submission, and outcome tracking; templates are versioned and larger requests can require board approval (`specs/012-integration-comptable/spec/spec.md`; `specs/017-module-subventions/spec/spec.md`).
- **Documents and compliance evidence:** The document generator merges data into editable templates, produces individual or batch documents, supports signature options, and archives document metadata and audit events. GDPR capabilities cover consent, access and deletion requests, processing records, cookies, and breach handling. The separate audit-trail design records security, administrative, financial, document, authentication, and data-mutation events, with anomaly detection and archival (`specs/016-generateur-documents/spec/spec.md`; `specs/019-gestion-rgpd/spec/spec.md`; `specs/020-audit-trail/spec/spec.md`).
- **Legal administration:** `api-juridique` is planned as a separate NestJS application using Firebase through `@linktogo/lk-db`, following `api-ae` and `api-mail` patterns. Its modules cover shared and association-customized templates, documents, obligations, deliberations, declarations, compliance snapshots, alerts, and legal monitoring. Tenant data is stored in `legal_` collections and scoped by `authGroupId`; system templates are global. The API is planned to use `ApiKeyGuard`, and mutations are limited to bureau roles. It calls `api-mail` for alerts and uses a daily scheduled job; deployment is constrained to one instance or a distributed lock to avoid duplicate scheduled runs (`specs/023-module-juridique/plan.md`; `specs/023-module-juridique/data-model.md`; `specs/023-module-juridique/research.md`).

The specifications describe these as separate capabilities. They do not establish a single shared runtime, a common authorization layer across all modules, or a deployed integration between every component.

## Invariants

1. Scope inventory, reservations, partners, partnerships, legal business data, and other association-owned records by `authGroupId`; do not expose records across association boundaries. Sources: `specs/006-gestion-stocks-inventaire/spec/spec.md`; `specs/007-partenariats-sponsors/spec/spec.md`; `specs/023-module-juridique/spec/spec.md`.
2. Recalculate inventory availability against existing reservation dates and quantities; detect conflicts, and retain approval, pickup, return, and condition states for loans. Source: `specs/006-gestion-stocks-inventaire/spec/spec.md`.
3. Track each partnership counterparty’s expected and completed quantity, deadline, status, and any required proof. Include completed deliverables and available visibility metrics in partner reports. Source: `specs/007-partenariats-sponsors/spec/spec.md`.
4. Restrict each official vote to eligible voters and its active period. Do not expose voter identities in secret-vote results; retain proxy records that identify who voted for whom. Invalidate a member’s proxy if that member casts their own vote. Source: `specs/008-votes-sondages/spec/spec.md`.
5. Keep quick polls distinct from official votes: do not apply quorum or proxy rules to polls, and make their results immediate. Source: `specs/008-votes-sondages/spec/spec.md`.
6. Label every executive-dashboard forecast with the specified historical-data disclaimer, and preserve drill-down from KPI cards to underlying detail. Source: `specs/009-tableau-bord-executif/spec/spec.md`.
7. Store workflow definitions and workflow executions separately, and preserve the trigger, state, steps, and context for each execution. Validate incoming webhook HMAC signatures and retry outgoing webhook delivery. Source: `specs/011-workflows-automatises/spec/spec.md`.
8. Require manual validation for proposed bank-statement matches. Retain OCR confidence and review indicators; do not treat OCR output as a confirmed transaction without review. Source: `specs/012-integration-comptable/spec/spec.md`.
9. Run bulk document generation asynchronously. Do not discard signature certificates or document audit metadata when archiving generated documents. Source: `specs/016-generateur-documents/spec/spec.md`.
10. Keep grant applications and their documents, requested and awarded amounts, and funder feedback associated with the application record. Filter opportunity alerts by the association profile and configured search criteria. Source: `specs/017-module-subventions/spec/spec.md`.
11. Keep necessary cookie consent true. Before deleting or anonymizing personal data, check applicable legal retention obligations; do not treat a deletion request as permission for unconditional erasure. Source: `specs/019-gestion-rgpd/spec/spec.md`.
12. Preserve audit-log immutability and integrity protections: the design calls for encrypted, hash-chained records with signed timestamps, recent storage in Firestore, and archival in Cloud Storage. Apply retention by audit category. Source: `specs/020-audit-trail/spec/spec.md`.
13. In `api-juridique`, require `authGroupId` for tenant operations, keep system templates global, and prevent access by members outside the tenant. Reserve official-document creation, modification, and validation for President, Secretary, and Treasurer; give other board members and members read-only access to archived documents. Source: `specs/023-module-juridique/spec/spec.md`; `specs/023-module-juridique/data-model.md`.
14. Keep legal API mutations behind the planned bureau-role check and protect controllers with `ApiKeyGuard`. Keep production secrets in Google Secret Manager. Make legal-alert delivery failure non-blocking only after the specified retries and logging. Source: `specs/023-module-juridique/plan.md`; `specs/023-module-juridique/quickstart.md`; `specs/023-module-juridique/research.md`.
15. Prevent duplicate legal scheduled jobs: deploy one instance or provide a distributed lock before relying on the NestJS cron process. Source: `specs/023-module-juridique/plan.md`; `specs/023-module-juridique/tasks.md`.

## Drift and superseded decisions

This is the risky part of the document: most domain sources are undated feature specifications and describe intended capabilities, not verified runtime behavior. Treat their proposed collections, endpoints, workflows, retention periods, and controls as design requirements to check against the code—not proof that those controls are implemented or deployed.

- **Legal module readiness is disputed.** `specs/023-module-juridique/checklists/requirements.md` gives the feature specification 1/16 (6%) and says it is not ready for planning, recommending a narrower first release and manual JOAFE/e-Préfecture flows if APIs are unavailable. The later `spec.md` records clarifications that choose the full seven-subsystem scope, while `plan.md` and `tasks.md` proceed with that scope; all 46 tasks in `tasks.md` are checked. That task status does not reconcile the quality finding or independently verify the implementation. Before changing legal scope or relying on an external legal integration, verify the application and ask whether the full clarified scope remains approved.
- **Legal authorization has a concrete boundary.** The legal design reserves official document creation, modification, and validation to President, Secretary, and Treasurer. The plan describes mutations more generally as restricted to “bureau” roles. Do not assume those role sets are equivalent; confirm the intended mapping before changing authorization.
- **The legal integration plan treats external failures as non-blocking only after retry and logging.** Do not silently omit errors or assume an external service is available. Research and quickstart say email-alert failure should be logged after retries; they do not claim an alert was delivered.
- **No supplied record establishes that inventory, partnerships, voting, executive dashboard, automated workflows, advanced accounting, document generation, grants, GDPR, or audit-trail designs are implemented.** Their specs give rollout phases or requirements, but no completion status.
- **The specifications name differing retention periods for different data categories.** For example, GDPR audit logs are specified at three years, while the audit-trail design gives category-specific periods, including ten years for financial logs and five years for administrative logs. Treat retention as category-specific, and do not collapse these requirements into one global duration.

## Open questions

- Which of the undated specifications have shipped, and where are their authoritative implementations, rules, and deployment configurations? The supplied records do not answer this.
- Does “bureau roles” in the legal API plan mean exactly President, Secretary, and Treasurer, or a broader role set? What should happen when the association’s role vocabulary does not map cleanly to those offices?
- Is the full seven-subsystem `api-juridique` scope still intended for v1 despite the requirements checklist’s recommendation to narrow it? Which JOAFE and e-Préfecture interactions must remain manual?
- Is legal monitoring limited to RSS feeds from Légifrance and associations.gouv.fr, as the research proposes, and what freshness or failure guarantees are expected?
- How should legal scheduled work be made single-run in production: enforce one instance, add a distributed lock, or use another approved mechanism?
- Which retention rule takes precedence when a record is covered by both the GDPR specification and the audit-trail category policy? The sources list durations but do not define a cross-module precedence procedure.
- What evidence of human review is required for bank reconciliation and OCR, and who may approve expense claims at each step? The accounting spec specifies manual validation and a responsible-user-then-treasurer workflow but leaves detailed authority and evidence requirements unstated.
- What are the authoritative thresholds for board approval of grant applications, and who may change them? The grants spec makes the threshold configurable but does not set its value or configuration authority.
- Which KPI definitions, anomaly thresholds, workflow templates, and sponsor placements are canonical, and who may configure them? The relevant specs describe customization but do not settle ownership or defaults.
